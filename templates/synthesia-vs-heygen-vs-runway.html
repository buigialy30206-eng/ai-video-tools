The Short Answer

Synthesia is best for professional avatar presenters, HeyGen for cloning yourself, and Runway for generating original footage from text. They're not competitors — they solve three different jobs, and picking the wrong one is the most expensive mistake you can make.

Synthesia: The Professional Standard

Synthesia is built for one thing — turning a script into a polished presenter video, fast.

What we liked

  • 230+ stock avatars, the most natural lip-sync we've tested
  • 140+ languages with accurate accents
  • Best-in-class for corporate training and product explainers

What holds it back

  • No real text-to-video (you're limited to avatars)
  • Priced for businesses, not casual creators
  • Custom avatar requires a paid plan

Verdict

If you need professional training or explainer videos at scale, this is the tool. If you're a YouTuber who wants to generate footage, it's the wrong pick.

HeyGen: The Creator's Cloning Machine

HeyGen's claim to fame is avatar cloning — upload a 2-minute video, get a digital clone that speaks 40+ languages. It's why faceless YouTube channels exploded.

What we liked

  • The best avatar cloning we've seen — shockingly realistic
  • Built-in video translation (talk in English, output Spanish)
  • Strong templates for short-form content

What holds it back

  • Cloning quality depends on your source footage
  • Less "enterprise polish" than Synthesia for corporate use
  • Credit system can feel limiting on lower tiers

Verdict

If you want to clone yourself or run a faceless channel, HeyGen is the clear winner. Creators pick it over Synthesia for exactly this reason.

Runway: The Creative Generator

Runway is a completely different animal — type a prompt, get a video clip (see the no-camera workflow). Its Gen-3 model produces cinematic footage that creators use for music videos, ads, and film work.

What we liked

  • The most powerful text-to-video generation available
  • Genuinely cinematic output for creative projects
  • Strong video editing and VFX tools built in

What holds it back

  • Not a presenter tool — no avatars at all
  • Higher learning curve than "type and get avatar"
  • Generation can be slow and expensive on heavy use

Verdict

If you generate original footage for creative work, Runway is unmatched. If you want a person speaking to camera, look elsewhere.

So Which One Do You Need?

Choose Synthesia if: you make training, onboarding, or explainer videos and need a polished presenter.

Choose HeyGen if: you're a creator who wants to clone yourself or scale a faceless channel.

Choose Runway if: you generate cinematic footage from prompts for ads, music videos, or film.

Choose two if: you want a presenter and original footage — the two categories genuinely complement each other.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Synthesia better than HeyGen?

For corporate training and explainer videos, Synthesia is more polished. For creators who want to clone themselves or run a faceless YouTube channel, HeyGen is the better choice thanks to its superior avatar cloning.

Can Runway make avatar videos?

No. Runway generates original footage from text prompts but has no avatar or talking-presenter feature. If you need a person speaking to camera, use Synthesia or HeyGen instead.

Which AI video tool is cheapest?

Runway and Pika start the lowest, at around $8-12 per month. Synthesia starts at $22 per month and HeyGen at $24 per month, reflecting their avatar and presenter features.

Can I use Synthesia and HeyGen together?

Yes. Many creators pair an avatar tool with a text-to-video tool — using one for presenter content and the other for b-roll and creative footage. The two categories complement rather than compete.

Do I need both an avatar tool and a text-to-video tool?

Not at first. Start with whichever solves your primary need — an avatar tool for presenter videos, or a text-to-video tool for original footage. Add a second tool only when your workflow genuinely requires both, since the two categories complement rather than replace each other.
